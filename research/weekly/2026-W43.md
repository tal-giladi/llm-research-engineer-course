### Training–inference mismatch in LLM RL (TitanRL batch-invariant mode, vLLM batch invariance, TIS) — ADD

- **Candidates:** C-20261025-01 (class A; the TitanRL part)
1. **Significant?** Yes. Every RL lesson in Modules 16–17 assumes `old_logprobs` describe the sampling distribution. In every production stack the sampler and the trainer are different programs whose probabilities differ (reduction order, batch-dependent kernels, precision, sampling transforms, MoE routing). An RL engineer who does not know this cannot tell an algorithm change from a numerics change.
2. **Enough evidence?** Yes, well beyond the staged post. (a) TorchTitan/TitanRL: one model definition through vLLM, batch-invariant mode with bitwise-identical trainer/generator log-probs in supported on-policy configurations **(company claim)**, TorchTitan used as backend by verl and Miles (https://pytorch.org/blog/torchtitan-update-new-sota-models-performance-at-scale-titanrl/, read). (b) vLLM ships a documented batch-invariance mode across many releases, RL listed as a use case (https://docs.vllm.ai/en/stable/features/batch_invariance/, read). (c) verl implements decoupled/bypass rollout correction with token/sequence TIS and rejection (https://github.com/volcengine/verl/blob/main/docs/algo/rollout_corr_math.md, read). (d) Qwen team study, 30B MoE, IS-corrected policy gradient most stable, Routing Replay for MoE (https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.01374) **(company claim)**. (e) Independent papers: zero-mismatch reference showing mismatch alone can collapse training (https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.14220), Jackpot (ICLR 2026, https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.06107), AIS (https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.13907), Trust Region Masking (https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.23075). (f) Foundations: decoupled PPO, Hilton et al. NeurIPS 2022 (https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.00641); TIS by Yao, Liu et al. (NeurIPS 2025 listing, blog posts; named only, hosts unreachable) and the Thinking Machines batch-invariance post (2025-09-10; named only).
3. **Mature enough to teach?** Yes. The mechanism (floating-point non-associativity + batch-dependent kernels → off-policy samples → importance correction with truncation, or removing the mismatch with invariant kernels) has been stable since 2025 and is implemented in several stacks. The open questions (caps for very long agentic rollouts, correct-vs-remove trade-off) are labelled as research in the lesson.
4. **Where does it fit?** New lesson 16.4 after 16.3. Prerequisites all taught earlier: GRPO ratio/clip (16.1), RLVR loop (16.2), KL penalty and log-ratio estimator (15.2), non-associativity and deterministic algorithms (07.3), bf16 (08.3), temperature/top-k/top-p (06.3). MoE routing (12.4) and linear-attention kernels (12.5) appear only as named causes.
5. **Genuinely new?** Yes. `lookup "batch invariant"` and `lookup "nondeterminism"` find no course or registry match; 07.3 covers run-to-run determinism only; no lesson mentions a behavior policy distinct from `pi_old`. Distinct from the deferred `async-grpo-staleness-convergence` (policy lag, a different cause) and from F.2's GRPO fixes.
6. **Implement?** Yes. A toy split-K matmul shows batch variance and its removal exactly (bit-for-bit); TIS/sequence weights/rejection/K3 are a few lines checked by hand; an exactly enumerable KL-regularized bandit shows the uncorrected update converging to the wrong policy and IS reaching the analytical optimum, and that IS cannot fix top-k/top-p support mismatch.

**Decision:** ADD
**Reason:** moved well beyond one framework post: implemented in vLLM, verl and TorchTitan, used by the Qwen team, and studied by several independent papers (incl. ICLR/NeurIPS). It teaches a durable concept that every RL lesson relies on and the course did not teach. Added as new lesson 16.4; 16.1–16.3 and `llmre/rl/grpo.py` are unchanged.

### C-20261019-01 · Prism Attention: proposal-refined cross-layer index sharing fixes sparse-attention's layer-drift problem, with a NeurIPS 2026 acceptance and a 3.4x decode speedup at 128K

- **Class:** A
- **Date discovered:** 2026-10-19
- **Date published:** 2026-10-16 (v1)
- **Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22985 (verified resolves; v1, submitted 16 Oct 2026, comments field states "Published as a main conference paper at NeurIPS 2026" — independently corroborated by the NeurIPS 2026 virtual program listing, https://neurips.cc/virtual/2026/poster/153474, verified resolves)
- **Organization/researchers:** Zhenxu Tian, Kebin Liu, Zhengwu Yang, Yi Su, Qingqing Dang, Yanlin Sha, Kaipeng Deng, Dianhai Yu, Yanjun Ma, Juntao Li, Min Zhang (affiliations not stated on the abstract page; several author names match Baidu's PaddlePaddle/inference-systems research group from other publications, not independently confirmed here).
- **Category:** attention / efficiency (training-free, cross-layer sparse-attention index sharing for long-context decoding)
- **What changed:** Token-level sparse attention for long-context decoding needs per-layer top-k index selection, but recomputing those indices at every layer erodes the efficiency win. The standard fix shares indices computed at a few "anchor" layers across the rest. Prism Attention's authors show this is a genuine approximation error, not a free lunch: the exact top-k set drifts layer to layer, concentrated at the edges of the previously selected region, even though a stable "core" of high-attention tokens persists. Prism replaces rigid cross-layer reuse with a coarse-to-fine, proposal-refined scheme that keeps per-layer adaptability while still sharing most of the computation that made index sharing efficient in the first place.
- **Technical summary:** Prism proposes a coarse candidate set once (cheaply, reusing upstream structure) and then cheaply refines it per layer at the edges where the authors' analysis shows drift concentrates, instead of either recomputing full top-k every layer (expensive) or freezing indices at anchor layers (increasingly wrong as depth grows). The abstract reports a 3.4x end-to-end decoding speedup over FlashAttention at 128K context length (a NeurIPS-listing search independently found a wider-range figure of "2.6x-7.5x kernel speedup over FlashAttention at 128K," consistent with the abstract's end-to-end number being a conservative point estimate within that range) and states the method reaches state-of-the-art results among mainstream sparse-attention methods on reasoning and long-context benchmarks; no dataset names or accuracy deltas were available from the abstract alone.
- **Why it might matter:** The course currently has no sparse-attention lesson at all — the registry's two closest topics, DoSA (research/deferred/sparse-attention-indexer-dosa.md) and PISA (research/deferred/block-sparse-attention-pisa.md), are both still WAIT/single-paper and address different sub-problems (certificate-based top-k scoring; hierarchical block-sparse O(N log N) scoring). Prism is the first candidate in this cluster with independent, primary-source confirmation of a top-tier peer-reviewed venue (not just the authors' own comments-field claim), and it isolates a genuinely instructive mechanism — "cross-layer sharing works because attention has a stable core, and fails at the edges as the model goes deeper" — that would make a clean worked example once the course has a sparse-attention lesson to put it (and/or DoSA/PISA) in.
- **Evidence of adoption:** Peer-reviewed acceptance at NeurIPS 2026 (independently verified via the conference's own program listing, not just the paper's self-reported comments field) — a real evidentiary upgrade over a bare arXiv preprint, though still a single paper with no reported third-party reproduction or production deployment.
- **Major organizations using it:** None identified; authors' affiliation not stated on the paper itself.
- **Open-source implementation:** none found (checked the arXiv abstract page and the NeurIPS 2026 virtual poster page; neither lists a code repository).
- **Paper:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22985
- **Code:** none found
- **Relationship to existing course material:** New topic, but in the same problem space as two existing WAIT topics. Checked via `lookup "sparse attention cross-layer index sharing"` (no matches) and a direct grep of `lessons/`/`research/` for "index sharing" / "Prism Attention" (no matches). Would bear on a future sparse-attention lesson alongside/instead of DoSA and PISA if the weekly review decides the course needs one; flagging the cluster (3 single-paper sparse-attention mechanisms now pending, none taught) as a possible gap-review question, similar to how 10-18 flagged the quantization gap for RoleQ.
- **Potential course lesson:** If/when the course adds a sparse-attention lesson (natural fit after Module 12's attention variants), Prism's layer-drift analysis is a strong motivating numerical example for "why cross-layer index sharing isn't free," contrasted with DoSA's certificate-based scoring and/or PISA's hierarchical block-sparse approach.
- **Confidence:** medium-high — independently confirmed top-tier peer review is a real evidentiary upgrade over the single-paper norm, but still no code release, no third-party reproduction, and no production adoption.
- **Recommendation:** review for ADD/WAIT-cluster decision at the next weekly cycle — specifically whether the sparse-attention cluster (Prism + the already-deferred DoSA + PISA) has now accumulated enough distinct, well-evidenced mechanisms to justify a new module-12 lesson, rather than evaluating Prism alone.

### C-20261019-02 · Zephon: elastic deterministic data loading for online, stateful foundation-model pipelines (PyTorch blog + TorchTitan integration)

- **Class:** A
- **Date discovered:** 2026-10-19
- **Date published:** 2026-10-02 (paper, v1); 2026-10-16 (PyTorch blog post and TorchTitan integration)
- **Source:** https://pytorch.org/blog/bringing-elastically-deterministic-data-loading-to-torchtitan-with-zephon/ (verified resolves, PyTorch's own official engineering blog, dated 2026-10-16)
- **Organization/researchers:** DatologyAI (Maximilian Böther, Josh Wills, Ties Robroek, Sonnet Xu, Paul Burstein, Daniel Zayas, Cody Blakeney, Siddharth Joshi, Haoli Yin, Rishabh Adiga, Haakon Mongstad, Luke Merrick, Pratyush Maini, Ari Morcos, Matthew Leavitt, Bogdan Gaza) with Ana Klimovic (ETH Zurich, a recognized ML-systems researcher — co-authorship independently plausible from the paper's author list, not re-confirmed via a separate ETH page). Published jointly with the PyTorch team (official blog) and integrated into Meta's TorchTitan.
- **Category:** distributed/efficient training (elastic data loading; reproducibility of online, stateful data pipelines under changing GPU/worker counts)
- **What changed:** Modern pretraining data pipelines tokenize, pack, and mix data on the fly rather than reading from a fixed, pre-materialized, indexable corpus — which is exactly what breaks determinism when a run resumes on a different number of GPUs (e.g. after a hardware fault) or when ablating a data-mixture change across different GPU counts. Zephon is a data-loading library that gives online, stateful pipelines "elastic determinism": the same global batch sequence (verified via per-step SHA-256 hashes) regardless of how many GPUs/workers are used, while still doing all the tokenize/pack/shuffle/mix work inside the training job (no throwaway offline-materialized copy of the corpus).
- **Technical summary:** Three composable mechanisms: (1) **accumulators make decisions, workers do the work** — each operator splits into a single-threaded, order-preserving accumulator (e.g. deciding which sequences get packed together) and a stateless function parallelized across interchangeable workers, so parallelism is a pure performance knob with no effect on output; (2) **lanes instead of ranks** — the data stream is split into a fixed number of logical "lanes," each independently deterministic, and lanes are redistributed across however many data-parallel ranks exist at runtime, rather than splitting work directly by rank; (3) **flush-and-replay instead of saving state** — checkpoints record only which input chunks were in flight since the last periodic "flush sentinel," so checkpoint size (a few MiB, per the authors) stays bounded regardless of how far into the run the checkpoint is taken, and restoring replays from the last flush rather than serializing full pipeline state. Reported results: resuming a 1B-parameter TorchTitan model's training on 8/32/64 GPUs from a 16-GPU checkpoint produces loss curves that overlay exactly (matching SHA-256 batch hashes) with Zephon, versus visibly divergent curves with a standard loader; training the same model from scratch at 8/16/32/64 GPUs with a standard loader produced DCLM Core v1 scores varying by up to 0.82 points and FineWeb-suite scores by up to 0.64 points purely as a function of GPU count (roughly 4x the thresholds DCLM/FineWeb's own authors treat as meaningful for data-curation comparisons) — with Zephon, that spread fell to 0.05 and 0.011 points respectively. On a full vision-language pipeline, Zephon matched the throughput of Google Grain (the fastest loader the authors tested) while adding the elastic-determinism guarantee neither Grain nor Mosaic Streaming nor Megatron-Energon provides for online, stateful (pack-on-the-fly) pipelines specifically.
- **Why it might matter:** This is a concrete, teachable answer to a reproducibility failure mode the course's data/distributed-training modules don't currently name: that an ablation comparing two data mixtures can silently be measuring GPU count instead of the intended change, with an effect size the authors show exceeds the noise floor the field's own benchmark authors use. The accumulator/lane/flush-replay decomposition is a clean, from-scratch-teachable mechanism (not just "use this library"), and it ships with a real open-source TorchTitan integration (https://github.com/datologyai/torchtitan-zephon) plus the standalone library (https://github.com/datologyai/zephon, Apache 2.0, 76 stars at time of check) — a genuine, if modest, adoption signal beyond a bare paper.
- **Evidence of adoption:** Published on PyTorch's own official engineering blog (primary source, company/foundation-backed — labeled per protocol), with a working TorchTitan integration (Meta's official pretraining framework) that the blog post walks through directly, not merely linked.
- **Major organizations using it:** DatologyAI (originating company); integrated with Meta's TorchTitan; co-developed with ETH Zurich. No evidence yet of adoption by a frontier lab's production training run beyond the TorchTitan integration itself.

- **Technical summary:** Per the paper (full abstract not independently reproduced here due to a 125-character quoting limit on the fetch tool, but corroborated by the comments field and summary): orthogonalization helps when the gradient is far from low-rank with high curvature along its largest singular directions; PolarGD can reach the minimizer in one step under conditions gradient descent cannot match; PolarGD is rotation-invariant (unlike plain sign descent); and, over many steps, PolarGD behaves like gradient descent preconditioned by the current iterate, so tiny singular values of the iterate slow it down relative to gradient descent — exactly the regime where Muon's incomplete (few-iteration) Newton-Schulz approximation, which handles tiny singular values more like gradient descent than true orthogonalization does, converges *faster* than exact orthogonalization would.
