### C-20261108-03 · Core Automation: cross-cluster "compute island" training (DiLoCo-family survey + two-site 32B MoE case study + butterfly mixing revisited)

- **Class:** B
- **Date discovered:** 2026-11-08
- **Date published:** 2026-11-02
- **Source:** https://www.coreauto.com/hitchhikers-guide-to-training-on-compute-islands — **not independently fetched by this session**: this host is blocked by this session's egress policy. Reviewed from a text-only copy Tal saved and provided directly (the copy is dated "scraped 2026-11-08" and notes images/diagrams/downloads were omitted). Author/company identity corroborated via a secondary source found by web search: a Nov 5 2026 podcast listing (https://podwise.ai/episodes/9324775) describing Rohan Anil as co-founder of Core Automation, discussing "compute islands."
- **Organization/researchers:** Rohan Anil (Core Automation, co-founder; the podcast listing says "former Anthropic researcher" — not independently confirmed beyond that one secondary source).
- **Category:** distributed
- **What changed:** Argues frontier-scale training can span multiple geographically separate, heterogeneous GPU clusters ("compute islands") instead of requiring one colocated cluster, combining (a) a DiLoCo-lineage inner/outer-optimizer scheme (fragment-wise, staggered, asynchronous-tolerant sync every 50 steps; Distributed-Shampoo-style local optimizer; stochastic-rounding compression + error feedback) and (b) a revival of "butterfly mixing" (Zhao & Canny) — pairwise XOR-indexed exchanges among learners over 3 rounds — as a drop-in replacement for all-reduce.
- **Technical summary:** Self-reported case study: a ~32B-param sparse MoE (256 experts, top-8 routing, no shared experts) trained across 160×B300 (US) and 160×GB300 (Asia), ~9,000 miles apart, over a 25 Gbps bidirectional link, reaching ≈0.2525 bits/byte vs. 0.2549 bpb for standard colocated training at matched compute — a single run, no seeds/error bars. Separately, 64 GB300 GPUs split into 8 independent 8-GPU learner cells using butterfly mixing (pairwise exchange in 3 stages: i⊕1, i⊕2, i⊕4) "matched the baseline" with no numeric table given. No code, benchmark harness, or model released; no third-party reproduction of either result.
- **Why it might matter:** `research/tools/research.py lookup` for "compute islands", "DiLoCo", and "butterfly mixing" all return no matches — this whole topic area (low-communication / cross-datacenter distributed training) is absent from the course and the registry. Module 09 ("Distributed training") currently covers only synchronous, colocated methods (09.1 DDP/all-reduce, 09.2 ZeRO/FSDP, 09.3 tensor/pipeline/expert parallelism) — nothing on local-update/federated-style methods or decentralized model-parallel training across unreliable peers.
- **Evidence of adoption:** This post's own case study is unverified and self-reported (company claim). But the method family it builds on has 3 years of independent, repeated reproduction across organizations: DiLoCo (Google DeepMind, arXiv 2311.08105, Nov 2023 — verified directly by this review: 500x less communication with 8 workers on C4, matching the post's description); OpenDiLoCo (Prime Intellect, open-sourced, scaled to 1.1B, a real run across 3 countries/2 continents); INTELLECT-1 (10B model, 1T tokens, 5 countries/3 continents, up to 112 H100s); Streaming DiLoCo (DeepMind follow-up). Project Suncatcher's Oct 30 2026 "first AI response from orbit" (Gemma 4 haiku) claim, used in the post's framing, is independently confirmed via Google's own blog post and multiple outlets. Not independently verified by this review: the rest of the post's history table (SWARM, DisTrO/DeMo, SparseLoCo, Pluralis Node0/Agora, Covenant-72B, Decoupled DiLoCo, Factored Gossip DiLoCo, the Oct 2026 cross-facility HPC demo).
- **Major organizations using it:** Google DeepMind (DiLoCo origin); Prime Intellect (OpenDiLoCo, INTELLECT-1); Pluralis (Node0, Agora, named in the post, not independently checked here); Core Automation (this post's two-site case study — company claim for the specific numbers).
- **Open-source implementation:** none found for this specific case study; OpenDiLoCo (an earlier, smaller-scale, independent implementation of the underlying DiLoCo method) is open-source per Prime Intellect's own blog.
- **Paper:** none for this post (company blog, not a paper); underlying DiLoCo paper: https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.08105 (verified).
- **Code:** none found.
- **Relationship to existing course material:** Checked via `lookup "compute islands"`, `lookup "DiLoCo"`, `lookup "butterfly mixing"` (no hits) — new topic area, not a duplicate of module 09 (synchronous/colocated-only) or anything else in the registry.
- **Potential course lesson:** Strong candidate for a new 09.4 lesson ("Low-communication & cross-datacenter training": local-SGD/FedAvg from scratch → DiLoCo's inner/outer-optimizer split from scratch → a small, from-scratch butterfly-mixing numerical exercise using the XOR-pairing schedule this post states explicitly, directly reproducible at toy scale). Should teach from the independently-reproduced literature (DiLoCo, Streaming/Decoupled DiLoCo, SWARM) as the primary source, using this post only as a motivating, explicitly-labeled-(company claim) case study and the orbital framing as a hook, not as the technical foundation.
- **Confidence:** medium-high that the underlying topic area is real and worth teaching (multi-year, multi-organization, independently reproduced literature with real large-scale runs); low on this post's specific two-site 32B MoE numbers and the butterfly-mixing-matches-baseline claim, which are single-source, self-reported, unreproduced, from a company whose business plausibly benefits from the "stranded compute" narrative. ### C-20261108-04 · Inkling 2 Small (Thinking Machines): single joint multi-domain RL run replacing specialist RL + multi-teacher distillation; full-pipeline NVFP4; small interleaved vision encoder

- **Class:** B
- **Date discovered:** 2026-11-08
- **Date published:** 2026-11-05
- **Source:** https://thinkingmachines.ai/news/inkling-2-small/ and model card https://thinkingmachines.ai/model-card/inkling-2-small/ — **not fetched directly by this session** (host blocked by this session's egress policy). Recorded from web-search summaries of those pages plus secondary coverage: https://latent.space/p/ainews-inkling-2-small-17a-594b-trained-in-68-hours-on-12t-tokens-beating-muse-spark/ , https://www.unite.ai/thinking-machines-lab-launches-inkling-2-small-with-open-weights-pending/ , https://github.com/choco9966/Awesome-LLM-Technical-Reports/issues/6 , https://www.orcarouter.ai/blog/inkling-2-small-release
- **Organization/researchers:** Thinking Machines Lab
- **Category:** rl / numerics / multimodal
- **What changed:** (1) Post-training is one joint RL run across all domains (reported: 1,075 steps, >17M rollouts, ~9 days, 2,048 GPUs, >3T tokens), replacing the first Inkling's separate reasoning and agentic RL runs merged by multi-teacher on-policy distillation. Coding was 38.5% of rollouts but 65% of RL tokens. Rewards reportedly: unit tests, rubric judges, pairwise reward models, plus on-policy self-distillation for alignment (secondary source only). (2) Pretraining natively in NVFP4: 8.4T tokens in 68 h on GB300 NVL72 (company claim; "~12T" figures appear to include mid-training); a TML researcher reportedly says pre-, mid- and post-training including RL sampling ran in NVFP4. Recipe (rounding, Hadamard, BF16 islands) not disclosed. (3) ~292M Perception-Encoder-based vision encoder, 20×20 patches, 2×2 pooling, image tokens interleaved with text in the decoder.
- **Technical summary:** 594B-total / ~17B-active MoE, 48 layers, 8-of-384 expert routing, hybrid local/global attention (third-party spec site). Benchmarks (MMMU Pro, ScreenSpot Pro, CharXiv RQ, others) are vendor-reported against same-size open-weight models (Kimi K3, GLM-5.3); no independent evaluation and no ablation of the joint-run choice or the encoder. Weights closed; Apache-2.0 release pending safety testing.
- **Why it might matter:** A frontier-lab data point that directly contrasts with the method just added to 17.4 (multi-teacher on-policy distillation): the same lab moved from specialists+MOPD to one joint run. The rollout-share vs token-share gap is a real-world instance of the token-budget imbalance 17.4's extension teaches. End-to-end NVFP4 including RL sampling bears on 16.4 (training–inference mismatch) and the approved PTQ / mixed-precision material.
- **Evidence of adoption:** Company claims only; no technical report or paper found; no independent reproduction of the joint-run vs MOPD comparison. NVFP4 pretraining itself has independent literature (NVIDIA arXiv 2509.25149; outlier-dynamics arXiv 2602.02047).
- **Major organizations using it:** Thinking Machines Lab (this model).

- **Code:** none.
- **Relationship to existing course material:** 17.4 extension (multi-teacher on-policy distillation, added W45) — contrasting design choice; 16.4 (training–inference mismatch); mixed-precision lessons and the approved PTQ foundation (NVFP4); no vision-language coverage in the course.
