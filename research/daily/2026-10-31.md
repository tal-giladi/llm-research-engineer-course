### C-20261031-02 · There Is No Free Lunch in RLVR: a theoretical account of the pass@1-vs-pass@k tradeoff

- **Class:** B
- **Date discovered:** 2026-10-31
- **Date published:** 2026-10-29 (v1)
- **Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.39295 (verified resolves; v1, Thu 29 Oct 2026, cs.LG)
- **Organization/researchers:** Yinuo Liu (sole author; affiliation not stated on the abstract page).
- **Category:** RL/post-training (theoretical analysis of RLVR's pass@1/pass@k tradeoff)
- **What changed:** Models RLVR as an exponential tilt of the base policy over strategies shared across prompts, and argues from this that any update raising pass@1 must lower the success rate on some prompt at large k — i.e. the pass@1-vs-pass@k reversal the course's deferred `rl-sharpening-tax` topic documents empirically is, on this account, a structural cost of the RL update itself, not a transient training artifact. Derives a bound on the number of pass@k crossovers, a resulting pass@1/pass@k frontier that a constrained update traces exactly, and an entropy-collapse relationship with an identifiable exponent; proposes a diagnostic (reading the bound/an order-preserving test/the "price" off an evaluation log) usable without retraining.
- **Technical summary:** Author's own claims (19 pages, 4 figures); states a linked "code and verification package" with "34/34 automated checks," not independently re-run in this check. No independent reproduction found. No stated venue.
- **Why it might matter:** The course already has a WAIT topic, `research/deferred/rl-sharpening-tax.md` (next review 2026-11-29), covering the same empirical phenomenon (pass@k coverage loss from RL post-training) from one single-paper empirical source (arXiv 2610.01509, Meta-affiliated per HF, unconfirmed). This paper is an independent, differently-sourced (theoretical, not benchmark-empirical) account of the same phenomenon, which — if it holds up — would strengthen the case that the sharpening tax is a structural property of RLVR rather than an artifact of one training recipe, directly relevant to that topic's scheduled review.
- **Evidence of adoption:** None — single-author paper, two days old at discovery, no stated venue, no third-party reproduction.
- **Major organizations using it:** None identified; no stated affiliation.

This is new, independent evidence bearing on that WAIT topic, not a duplicate of its existing evidence.

### C-20261031-03 · Why Speculative Decoding Works Better Than Predicted on Sparse MoE Models

- **Class:** B
- **Date discovered:** 2026-10-31
- **Date published:** 2026-10-28 (v1)
- **Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38981 (verified resolves; v1, 28 Oct 2026, cs.LG; accepted to NeurIPS 2026 main track per the abstract page)
- **Organization/researchers:** Ekagra Ranjan, Komal K. Teru, Bharat Venkitesh, Acyr Locatelli (affiliations not stated on the abstract page).
- **Category:** inference/serving + MoE (empirical + predictive model of speculative decoding's interaction with MoE expert routing)
- **What changed:** Prior work predicted speculative decoding's speedup on MoE models peaks at intermediate batch sizes (expert-saturation argument). Testing 10 real sparse MoE models (30B-1T params, 8-512 experts), the authors find recent *fine-grained* MoE models break this pattern, showing higher speedups at **low** batch sizes instead. They attribute this to (a) MoE layers claiming a smaller share of runtime than their parameter share at low batch size (measured via H100 kernel profiling: higher MoE-kernel bandwidth utilization than attention, L2 cache absorbing repeated co-routed-expert reads) and (b) routing overlap across draft tokens reducing the number of unique experts actually activated during verification versus an independence assumption. They build a maximum-entropy Iterative Proportional Fitting (IPF) model that predicts unique-expert counts from routing-overlap statistics to <4% error.
- **Technical summary:** Authors' own measurements across 10 real production-scale MoE models; NeurIPS 2026 main-track acceptance is independent peer review of the method and claims (though not independent reproduction by a separate group).
- **Why it might matter:** Directly useful, actionable inference-serving knowledge for anyone deploying speculative decoding on fine-grained MoE models (an increasingly standard architecture family) — corrects a specific, previously load-bearing prediction in prior work with real multi-model measurement and a predictive model, not just an ablation.
- **Evidence of adoption:** Peer-reviewed (NeurIPS 2026 main track) but no third-party reproduction or production-stack adoption of the IPF predictive model found yet; three days old at discovery.
- **Major organizations using it:** None identified; authors' affiliations not stated on the abstract page.
- **Open-source implementation:** none found on the abstract page.
- **Paper:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38981
- **Code:** none found
- **Relationship to existing course material:** Checked via `lookup "speculative decoding draft model"` and `lookup "MoE expert saturation batch size inference"` (no hits either way) — the course does not currently teach speculative decoding at all, so this is a new topic area rather than an extension; it is, however, adjacent to the Nemotron 3.5 record above, which reports MTP-driven native speculative decoding numbers from a production system.
- **Potential course lesson:** Not yet actionable as a standalone addition since the course has no speculative-decoding lesson to extend; worth keeping alongside the Nemotron record as two independent data points if an inference-serving/speculative-decoding lesson is ever built.
- **Confidence:** medium — NeurIPS 2026 acceptance and a real 10-model empirical study with a validated predictive model, but no course material yet to attach it to and no third-party reproduction.
- **Recommendation:** monitor; revisit if the course adds a speculative-decoding lesson, or if the IPF model/finding is independently reproduced or adopted by a serving stack (vLLM/SGLang/TensorRT-LLM).

### C-20261031-04 · Multi-Token Prediction Done Right (Soft-MTP): fixing a gradient-interference flaw in standard MTP training

- **Class:** B
- **Date discovered:** 2026-10-31
- **Date published:** 2026-10-28 (v1)
- **Source:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38331 (verified resolves; v1, 28 Oct 2026, cs.LG)
- **Organization/researchers:** Jayden Teoh (sole author; affiliation not stated on the abstract page).
- **Category:** architecture/training (multi-token-prediction training objective; also bears on speculative decoding)
- **What changed:** Shows the standard MTP cross-entropy gradient decomposes into a KL self-distillation term (pulls each MTP head toward the next-token distribution, helps speculative decoding) and a low-signal-to-noise residual term that interferes with learning and worsens with scale. Proposes a "KL gradient-recovery trick" (straight-through gradient routing) that extracts the KL gradient from existing cross-entropy computations without materializing vocabulary-sized logit matrices, giving "Soft-MTP": a one-line change dropping the residual, with no added parameters or measurable overhead, compatible with both DeepSeek-style and Meta-style MTP.
- **Technical summary:** Author's own results from 167M to 1.2B parameters: Soft-MTP beats standard MTP on next-token perplexity, speculative-decoding speed, and zero-shot accuracy at every tested scale; improves math/code reasoning after fine-tuning at 1.2B; matches or exceeds direct KL-divergence minimization on most metrics. Code released and independently confirmed to exist: https://github.com/JaydenTeoh/Soft-MTP (MIT license, verified resolves; README documents the training/eval setup, checkpoints on Hugging Face, and a speculative-decoding eval script).
- **Why it might matter:** MTP is production-relevant (DeepSeek-V3's training objective, already in the course's capstone paper guide per `papers/index.md`; also the mechanism behind the Nemotron 3.5 record's native speculative decoding above) but the course has no dedicated lesson on MTP gradient mechanics. This is a concrete, code-backed, mechanistically clear fix (not just an ablation) with a plausible story (gradient decomposition) and multi-scale results, directly relevant if MTP is ever taught as its own mechanism rather than only cited inside the DeepSeek-V3 paper guide.
- **Evidence of adoption:** None beyond the author's own release — single author, three days old at discovery, no third-party reproduction, no stated venue, models tested only up to 1.2B parameters (small by production standards).
- **Major organizations using it:** None identified; independent/academic authorship, no stated affiliation.
- **Open-source implementation:** https://github.com/JaydenTeoh/Soft-MTP (verified: public repo, MIT license, runnable training/eval code, not independently re-run in this check).
- **Paper:** https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.38331
- **Code:** https://github.com/JaydenTeoh/Soft-MTP
- **Relationship to existing course material:** Checked via `lookup "multi-token prediction MTP"` (no hit) and by grepping `papers/index.md` directly: DeepSeek-V3's entry lists "Multi-token prediction training objective" as one of several capstone techniques but there is no dedicated MTP lesson or code module to extend — this is a new sub-topic, not a duplicate.
- **Potential course lesson:** If reproduced at larger scale or adopted by a production MTP implementation, a short from-scratch addition near the DeepSeek-V3 paper guide or module 12: the gradient-decomposition view of MTP and the one-line KL-recovery fix, using the released repo as a runnable toy example.
- **Confidence:** low-medium — a clean mechanistic argument with code and consistent multi-scale results, but single/unaffiliated author, small model scale (≤1.2B), no stated venue, no reproduction.
- **Recommendation:** monitor; worth an early code spot-check given the small, runnable released repo, but hold for independent reproduction or larger-scale validation before considering a course addition.
